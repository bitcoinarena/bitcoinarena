How your money is protected

The worst things that could happen, how likely each one is, what stops it, and what is left. Worst case first, in plain words.

Mainnet is not live. Bitcoin Arena runs on testnet today, with test coins that have no value. This page describes version 2.0 first: the protection Bitcoin Arena is built for. What the version running on testnet today, 1.9, already does is in its own section below.
Version 2.0

Where your money is, and who guards it

1. In your wallet E-cash in a federation: a group of guardians that hold the bitcoin together. No single guardian can spend it. Their bonds stand behind it.
2. Locked in a round Your stake waits in a lock that only the round's signers can pay out, and only by the result anyone can replay. If the round fails, the lock comes back to you within about a day.
3. Paid out Winnings land in your wallet as e-cash again. The 24 words you wrote down restore it on any device.

When a node is slashed

Every node that signs rounds or guards money puts up a bond: bitcoin it locks on the chain. Slashing takes that bond away. It happens only for these, and each one needs a proof anyone can check:

A false resultA signer signs a result the game did not produce. Anyone can replay the round; when the replay shows a different result, that signature is the proof. Every signer of the false result is slashed - also when the whole group lied together.
Two different resultsA signer signs two different results for the same order. The two signatures side by side are the proof, nothing needs to be replayed.
Confirming a false resultAn auditor checks a round and confirms its result. If that result is later proven false, the auditor's confirmation is the proof against the auditor.
A false payout tableThe seats that pay a round out sign a table of who gets what. A table that pays differently than the final result is the proof against every seat that signed it.
Releasing a bond without a proofFreeing a held bond to someone needs a valid proof. A node that signs such a release without one is slashed itself, and its whole bond goes to the protocol reserves.
A guardian walks awayA guardian of a federation stops working and does not hand over its place in time. Its bond pays the players of that federation.
Guardians stealEnough guardians sign together and take a federation's bitcoin. Their own signed history shows it: more e-cash paid out than was issued, or reserves spent with no signed withdrawal. The bonds of all its guardians pay every note at face value.

Mistakes that hurt nobody are not slashed:

Not slashed: being offlineA node that is not there for a day is simply left out of the draw until it is back. Its bond is untouched.
Not slashed: a round that stallsA seat named silent in failed rounds again and again is left out of the draw for up to 72 hours. Its bond is untouched; the players get their stake back.

How slashing works

1. A proof of cheating

Someone holds proof that a node cheated in one of the cases above: two signatures that contradict each other, or a signature that a replay of the round contradicts. Any node may file it.

2. The hold

The bond is moved to a hold on the chain. It is not spent yet and still counts as the node's. Nothing has been paid to anyone.

3. Two panels check

Two separate groups of up to seven bonded nodes each check the proof themselves. Only when two thirds and one more in each group sign - 5 of 7 - is the bond released. A node that signs without a valid proof is slashed for it.

4. Paid out

Every player the cheating harmed gets the real loss back first, once. The rest of the bond goes to the protocol reserves, which cover future losses.

Two things can stop the normal path, and each has its own way out:

There is no valid proof of cheating

Then the panels do not sign, and nobody else will either. The bond simply stays in the hold until its owner takes it back with the owner key - the 24 words. That is possible a day later at the earliest, and never before the bond's maturity date. The owner loses nothing.

The panels do not decide in time

For example, too many of their nodes are offline, so fewer than 5 of 7 sign. Then, after about two days, a backup group may act instead: the holders of the network's census key (explained below). Each of them checks the same proof of cheating itself and signs only if it holds. They pay out exactly as the panels would have: the players' loss first, the rest of the bond to the reserves.

So a slash with a real proof cannot get stuck, and a slash with a false proof cannot get through.

How can other nodes reach a node's bond?

A bond is bitcoin locked on the chain like in a box with several doors. Each door has its own lock and opens only with the keys written on it - Bitcoin checks this, nobody else. In Bitcoin's own words a door is called a leaf: one of the ways an output may be spent.

So other nodes can never simply take a bond. They reach it only through door 2, and door 2 needs two keys at once:

The node's own advance signature

Every time a node takes a seat in a round, it signs in advance: "if I cheat in this round, my bond may be moved into this round's hold". It signs one more when it puts up the bond, for the cases that are not about a round, such as a guardian walking away. Without it the bond cannot move, and it can only move into that hold - nowhere else.

The census key

The network's shared key (explained below). Its holders sign only after each of them has checked the proof of cheating.

Inside the hold

The other nodes again need a door of their own: the two panels with a valid proof (door A), or the backup group after two days (door C). Door B always stays open for the owner, so a bond nobody can prove anything against always comes back.

What is the census key?
What the census key is

The census key is one key that belongs to all bonded nodes of the network together. It is cut into pieces, and every node gets pieces in proportion to its bond. The key can sign only when nodes holding two thirds of the pieces sign together, so no single node - and no small group - can use it alone. A new census key is made at regular intervals, so the pieces follow the bonds as they change. It is used for just two things: to move a bond into its hold once a proof of cheating is checked, and as the backup group above.

Technical details: how a slash runs on the chain
The bond

A bond is a Taproot output. Taproot lets an output carry several alternative scripts - its leaves, the doors above - and a spend reveals only the one it uses. A bond has two: the recovery leaf (door 1) lets the owner key take it back after the bond's maturity height; the slash leaf (door 2) needs two signatures together: one the node made in advance for each round it sits in, which can only pay into that round's hold output, and one from the census key. So no one can take a bond anywhere else, not even the census key.

The proof of cheating

It is signed data anyone can check without trusting the filer: a signed result plus the replay of the round under its published game code; two signed results for one order; a signed confirmation of a result later proven false; a signed payout table that differs from the final result; a release signed with no valid proof behind it; or, for a federation, its own threshold-signed history showing more e-cash redeemed than issued, or a reserve output spent by no signed withdrawal.

The slashable offence, and the start

What sets everything off is the slashable offence itself, proven. Any node that holds the proof of cheating asks the census key's holders to sign; each holder checks the proof itself before it signs. Only then is the start made: the transaction that moves the bond through the slash leaf (door 2) into the hold output. The filer adds a coin for the fee.

The census key

It is one Schnorr key whose private part no one holds: it is split among a set of bonded nodes, drawn again every epoch, and a threshold of them must sign together. It can do only two things: sign a start, and pay out a hold after the long wait.

The hold

The hold output has no key path and three leaves. Release: after 6 blocks, two-thirds plus one of each panel - two groups of up to 7 nodes drawn from the census - sign together. Return: after 144 blocks (about a day) and the bond's maturity, the owner key alone. Fallback: after 288 blocks (about two days), the census key alone - the backup group, whose holders each check the proof before they sign. While the bond sits in its hold it still counts as the node's bond.

The payout

A release or a fallback pays a table that every signer computes the same way from public data: each harmed player's real loss once - paid into a safe federation by a carrier and on to the player's key - the filer's fee back at the start's own fee rate, and the rest to the protocol reserves. Its OP_RETURN names the proof's hash, the slash height and the table's hash, so anyone can check it against the proof.

Safety rules

Every panel member and holder builds the transaction from the chain itself and signs only its own bytes. A panel member who signs a release with no valid proof gives a proof against itself. The times are mainnet's; testnet uses shorter ones.

The worst cases

A round's signers lie about the result

live on testnet slash: comes with 2.0
What could happen
The signers of a round sign a result that the game did not produce, to pay the wrong player.
How likely
Low. It needs most of a randomly drawn group to agree to cheat, each with a bond at stake.
What stops it
Every round can be replayed by anyone. Auditors replay it, there is a window to object, a disputed round is run again, and the signers who lied lose their bond.
What is left
If 5 of the 7 signers lie and nobody objects within 10 minutes, the wrong result stands. A proof later still costs them their bonds.
DetailsReplay and objection: spec §13A, §13A.1. The slash for a false result: §5A.3, in 2.0.

A few seats stay silent to stop or void a round

comes with 2.0
What could happen
Some seats stop answering so a round cannot finish, again and again.
How likely
Medium. A seat only has to go quiet.
What stops it
A round waits and is run again by its seats. A seat named silent in 3 failed rounds within 72 hours is left out of the draw for up to 72 hours. Your stake comes back if the round cannot finish.
What is left
Three seats working together can still stop a round. A losing player then gets the stake back and the winner gets nothing extra. The stall rule takes repeaters out, but it does not yet cover the payout step.
DetailsRe-run window: §13C. The stall rule: §5A.3.

Settlement seats sign a different payout

comes with 2.0
What could happen
The seats that pay a round out sign a payout that differs from the result.
How likely
Low. It needs a threshold of seats, each with a bond.
What stops it
Every seat acknowledges the payout table it signs, and a false table is a proof that slashes those seats. The players get their real loss from the slashed bonds.
What is left
Five seats that acknowledge one payout and then sign another cannot be named, so no bond answers for that. It needs 5 of the 7, and a cap on what a round can hold keeps it small. This is still being decided.
DetailsAcknowledgements and table proofs: §5A.3.

Guardians are hacked or work together to steal

planned for 2.0
What could happen
Enough guardians of a federation sign together and take the bitcoin it holds.
How likely
Low. It needs a majority of a federation's guardians. Today on testnet most guardians are run by the project; spreading them over different machines and providers comes with 2.0.
What stops it
A federation may only hold as much as its guardians' bonds can cover. A theft leaves a mark in the federation's own signed history: more e-cash paid out than was ever issued, or a reserve spent with no signed withdrawal. Any seat that sees it stops deposits at once, and every note is paid at face value from the bonds of all its guardians. Updates reach only a few guardians of a federation at a time.
What is left
Your claim: you have 3 months to present your notes, and they are paid at once. Deposits made outside our apps and gateways past the cap may not be covered.
DetailsExposure cap, the reserve alarm, guardian bonds as insurance, staged updates and spread guardians: §23A.

A federation stops working

planned for 2.0
What could happen
A federation halts, is robbed, or its guardians leave.
How likely
Low to medium over years.
What stops it
The federation is wound up: every note presented is paid at face value, from the federation's escrow and its guardians' bonds. Your wallet claims for you automatically.
What is left
You have 3 months to claim. After that, what is left goes to the protocol reserves.
DetailsLiquidation and claims: §23A.5 (claim window 3 months).

A node is hacked

planned for 2.0
What could happen
Someone takes over the machine a node runs on.
How likely
Medium: machines get hacked.
What stops it
A node never holds a player's money, and its bond can only be taken back with its owner's 24 words, which are kept off the machine. A hacked node can sign no more than its own share of a round.
What is left
The node owner can lose the bond if the hacked node is slashed. Players lose nothing. If the node is a guardian and its federation is robbed, its bond pays the players.
DetailsThe owner key of a bond: §5A.3B.

Someone slashes a node falsely

comes with 2.0
What could happen
Someone tries to take a node's bond without a real offence.
How likely
Low.
What stops it
A slash first moves the bond to a hold where it still counts. Two independent panels must release it with a valid proof, and signing a release without one is itself slashable. If the proof does not hold up, the node takes its bond back by itself after about a day, never before its maturity.
What is left
The holders of most of the network's bonded capital, acting together, could take a bond that has not matured yet.
DetailsHold, panels, census key, return: §5A.3B.

A Lightning gateway misbehaves

live on testnet
What could happen
A gateway that moves money in and out over Lightning takes a payment and does not deliver it.
How likely
Low.
What stops it
Lightning payments are all or nothing: either the money arrives or it never leaves. Every federation has at least two gateways, and your wallet uses the other one.
What is left
A payment may fail and need to be tried again.

The website is changed

live on testnet known-good list: planned
What could happen
Someone changes the game page to steal from the wallet in your browser.
How likely
Low to medium.
What stops it
The page loads each game only if its files match the published hashes, and asks you before money leaves your wallet. The code is public. A list of known-good builds is planned.
What is left
A changed page you open before anyone notices.

Someone tries to pick a round's signers

live on testnet
What could happen
Someone tries to choose which nodes sign a round, to get their own nodes in.
How likely
Low.
What stops it
The signers are drawn from a Bitcoin block hash that comes after the round was published, so nobody knows it in advance. A cap on how much a round can hold keeps what a captured round could take small.
What is left
An owner with a large share of all bonds is drawn more often, in proportion.
DetailsBeacon after publication and stake ceiling: §5A.5, §5A.10.

You lose your phone or computer

live on testnet
What could happen
The device with your wallet is lost or broken.
How likely
Medium: it happens to everyone.
What stops it
Your 24 words restore your wallet and your notes on any device. A Nostr login does the same.
What is left
If you lose the device and the 24 words, that money is gone.

A bug in the software

live on testnet staged updates: planned
What could happen
A mistake in the code loses or locks money.
How likely
Medium: all software has bugs.
What stops it
Everything runs on testnet first, with test coins. The code is public. Updates will reach only a few guardians of a federation at a time, so a faulty release shows before it can do harm.
What is left
A bug nobody finds before mainnet.

What we cannot promise

  • If every guardian of a federation were hacked at once, nobody would hand out its signed history, and a theft would show only when withdrawals fail.
  • Three seats working together can stop a round, and give a losing friend the stake back.
  • Five seats that sign a different payout than they acknowledged cannot be named yet.
  • If you are away for more than 3 months after a federation stops, and you have lost your 24 words, that money is lost.
  • An owner with a large share of all bonds is drawn into more rounds. The more of the bonded capital one owner holds, the higher the chance that their nodes sign a round together.
  • Software can have bugs we have not found yet.

Version 1.9 - testnet today

What version 1.9 does today

Version 1.9 is what runs on testnet now. Much of the protection above is already there; slashing is not.

Live in 1.9Every round can be replayed by anyone. A false result can be objected to for 10 minutes and the round is then run again. A round that cannot finish gives your stake back. Signers are drawn from a block hash nobody knows in advance. Every federation has two gateways. Game files load only when they match their published hashes. Your 24 words restore your wallet.
Slashing in 1.9: proofs onlySeats already file the proofs of a false result and publish them, and every seat checks them and writes down what the slash would owe. But no bond is taken: a 1.9 bond cannot be moved by a proof. A node that lies loses the round, not its bond.
Not yet in 1.9Taking a bond, the hold and the two panels. Guardian bonds that pay the players after a theft. Leaving silent seats and absent nodes out of the draw. A cap on what a federation may hold. All of these come with version 2.0.
Until 2.0, Bitcoin Arena stays on testnet with coins that have no value. Mainnet starts with the protection of version 2.0, not with 1.9.
