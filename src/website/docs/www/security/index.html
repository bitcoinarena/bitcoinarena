How your money is protected

The worst things that could happen, how likely each one is, what stops it, and what is left. Worst case first, in plain words.

Mainnet is not live. Bitcoin Arena runs on testnet today, with test coins that have no value. Each card says whether its protection is live on testnet now, comes with version 2.0, or is planned.

Where your money is, and who guards it

1. In your wallet E-cash in a federation: a group of guardians that hold the bitcoin together. No single guardian can spend it. Their bonds stand behind it.
2. Locked in a round Your stake waits in a lock that only the round's signers can pay out, and only by the result anyone can replay. If the round fails, the lock comes back to you within about a day.
3. Paid out Winnings land in your wallet as e-cash again. The 24 words you wrote down restore it on any device.

The worst cases

A round's signers lie about the result

live on testnet slash: comes with 2.0
What could happen
The signers of a round sign a result that the game did not produce, to pay the wrong player.
How likely
Low. It needs most of a randomly drawn group to agree to cheat, each with a bond at stake.
What stops it
Every round can be replayed by anyone. Auditors replay it, there is a window to object, a disputed round is run again, and the signers who lied lose their bond.
What is left
If 5 of the 7 signers lie and nobody objects within 10 minutes, the wrong result stands. A proof later still costs them their bonds.
DetailsReplay and objection: spec §13A, §13A.1. The slash for a false result: §5A.3, in 2.0.

A few seats stay silent to stop or void a round

comes with 2.0
What could happen
Some seats stop answering so a round cannot finish, again and again.
How likely
Medium. A seat only has to go quiet.
What stops it
A round waits and is run again by its seats. A seat named silent in 3 failed rounds within 72 hours is left out of the draw for up to 72 hours. Your stake comes back if the round cannot finish.
What is left
Three seats working together can still stop a round. A losing player then gets the stake back and the winner gets nothing extra. The stall rule takes repeaters out, but it does not yet cover the payout step.
DetailsRe-run window: §13C, #1038, in 2.0. The stall rule: #1044, #1052, in 2.0. The free bet: #1044, #1045.

Settlement seats sign a different payout

comes with 2.0
What could happen
The seats that pay a round out sign a payout that differs from the result.
How likely
Low. It needs a threshold of seats, each with a bond.
What stops it
Every seat acknowledges the payout table it signs, and a false table is a proof that slashes those seats. The players get their real loss from the slashed bonds.
What is left
Five seats that acknowledge one payout and then sign another cannot be named, so no bond answers for that. It needs 5 of the 7, and a cap on what a round can hold keeps it small. This is still being decided.
DetailsAcknowledgements and table proofs: §5A.3.

Guardians are hacked or work together to steal

planned for 2.0
What could happen
Enough guardians of a federation sign together and take the bitcoin it holds.
How likely
Low. It needs a majority of a federation's guardians. Today on testnet most guardians are run by the project; spreading them over different machines and providers comes with 2.0.
What stops it
A federation may only hold as much as its guardians' bonds can cover. A spend nobody asked for raises an alarm, deposits stop, and every note is paid at face value from the bonds of all its guardians. Updates reach only a few guardians of a federation at a time.
What is left
Your claim: you have 3 months to present your notes, and they are paid at once. Deposits made outside our apps and gateways past the cap may not be covered.
DetailsExposure cap, ReserveAlarm, guardian bonds as insurance, staged updates, spread guardians: #1093.

A federation stops working

planned for 2.0
What could happen
A federation halts, is robbed, or its guardians leave.
How likely
Low to medium over years.
What stops it
The federation is wound up: every note presented is paid at face value, from the federation's escrow and its guardians' bonds. Your wallet claims for you automatically.
What is left
You have 3 months to claim. After that, what is left goes to the protocol reserves.
DetailsLiquidation and claims: §23A.5, #1093 (claim window 3 months).

A node is hacked

planned for 2.0
What could happen
Someone takes over the machine a node runs on.
How likely
Medium: machines get hacked.
What stops it
A node never holds a player's money, and its bond can only be taken back with its owner's 24 words, which are kept off the machine. A hacked node can sign no more than its own share of a round.
What is left
The node owner can lose the bond if the hacked node is slashed. Players lose nothing. If the node is a guardian and its federation is robbed, its bond pays the players.
DetailsOwner key for bonds: #1093 part 1.

Someone slashes a node falsely

comes with 2.0
What could happen
Someone tries to take a node's bond without a real offence.
How likely
Low.
What stops it
A slash first moves the bond to a hold where it still counts. Two independent panels must release it with a valid proof, and signing a release without one is itself slashable. If the proof does not hold up, the node takes its bond back by itself after about a day, never before its maturity.
What is left
The holders of most of the network's bonded capital, acting together, could take a bond that has not matured yet.
DetailsHold, panels, census key, return: §5A.3B.

A Lightning gateway misbehaves

live on testnet
What could happen
A gateway that moves money in and out over Lightning takes a payment and does not deliver it.
How likely
Low.
What stops it
Lightning payments are all or nothing: either the money arrives or it never leaves. Every federation has at least two gateways, and your wallet uses the other one.
What is left
A payment may fail and need to be tried again.

The website is changed

live on testnet known-good list: planned
What could happen
Someone changes the game page to steal from the wallet in your browser.
How likely
Low to medium.
What stops it
The page loads each game only if its files match the published hashes, and asks you before money leaves your wallet. The code is public. A list of known-good builds is planned.
What is left
A changed page you open before anyone notices.

Someone tries to pick a round's signers

live on testnet
What could happen
Someone tries to choose which nodes sign a round, to get their own nodes in.
How likely
Low.
What stops it
The signers are drawn from a Bitcoin block hash that comes after the round was published, so nobody knows it in advance. A cap on how much a round can hold keeps what a captured round could take small.
What is left
An owner with a large share of all bonds is drawn more often, in proportion.
DetailsBeacon after publication and stake ceiling: §5A.5, §5A.10.

You lose your phone or computer

live on testnet
What could happen
The device with your wallet is lost or broken.
How likely
Medium: it happens to everyone.
What stops it
Your 24 words restore your wallet and your notes on any device. A Nostr login does the same.
What is left
If you lose the device and the 24 words, that money is gone.

A bug in the software

live on testnet staged updates: planned
What could happen
A mistake in the code loses or locks money.
How likely
Medium: all software has bugs.
What stops it
Everything runs on testnet first, with test coins. The code is public. Updates will reach only a few guardians of a federation at a time, so a faulty release shows before it can do harm.
What is left
A bug nobody finds before mainnet.

What we cannot promise

  • If every guardian of a federation and every seat were hacked at once, nobody could raise the alarm, and the bonds might not pay.
  • Three seats working together can stop a round, and give a losing friend the stake back.
  • Five seats that sign a different payout than they acknowledged cannot be named yet.
  • If you are away for more than 3 months after a federation stops, and you have lost your 24 words, that money is lost.
  • An owner with a large share of all bonds is drawn into more rounds. The more of the bonded capital one owner holds, the higher the chance that their nodes sign a round together.
  • Software can have bugs we have not found yet.
