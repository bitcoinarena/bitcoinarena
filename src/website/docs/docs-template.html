Bitcoin Arena docs

Federated networkShared custodySettles in BitcoinNostr native

Bitcoin Arena runs games and prediction markets played for bitcoin. No company holds the money: a federation of independent nodes takes the stakes, decides the results and pays the winners.

Bitcoin Arena runs on Bitcoin testnet4 with test coins. Mainnet is not live.

Glossary

Round
One match of a game, or one market. It has a start, an entry close, a stake and a round id.
Entry
A player's place in a round, with their stake.
Node
One running arena-node server, also called a seat.
Bond
Bitcoin a node locks on chain to get a seat. Its signed advertisement goes in the same transaction. See Bond and advertisement.
Census
All confirmed bonds that are old enough. Committees are drawn from it.
Committee
The 7 nodes drawn to run a round. 5 of 7 must agree on its result.
Federation
The Fedimint federation that holds the stakes. Its guardians are nodes.
E-cash
The federation's money, kept in your browser wallet.
Stake lock
Holds your stake until the result opens it. See Stake locks.
Settlement
The one transaction that pays a round's winners.
Manifest
The developer's signed description of a game: rules, files and payout. Its hash is the game id.
Replayed game
The nodes replay each player's inputs to get the score (Tick Tock, Next Block).
Attested game
A prediction market whose outcome is signed by attestors the developer named (Bitcoin Up or Down).
Head-to-head game
Players hide a move and reveal it after entries close (Rock Paper Scissors).
Template
A market whose rounds each ask their own question. See Event-market templates.
Leaderboard
One game's ranking over recent days. See Leaderboard.
Donation
A share of a paid stake for the game's and the protocol's developers. See dev client.
Protocol reserves
The network's own fund, 2% of every paid round, paid out only to proposals the nodes vote for. See Protocol reserves.
Objection window
10 minutes after a result in which anyone can prove it wrong. See When a node cheats.

Start playing

It runs in your browser. Nothing to install, no account.

  1. Open testnet.bitcoinarena.io. The lobby lists the open rounds.
  2. Make a wallet: create a new one (back up its 24 words later in Settings), restore one from its 24 words, or log in with Nostr.
    Keep your 24 words or Nostr key safe. Nobody can recover your wallet without them.
  3. Deposit: pick an amount and pay the Lightning invoice, by QR code or from a wallet connected with Nostr Wallet Connect. On testnet the page links a test wallet with a faucet.
  4. Join a round. The lobby shows each round's stake and federation. Your stake is locked in the federation, and play starts once the nodes have checked the lock.
  5. Get paid. Winnings and refunds land in your wallet on their own, shown as pending until redeemed. Withdraw any time with a Lightning invoice.

Settings

The gear opens Settings: balance, Deposit, Withdraw, Move, your lobby name (up to 32 characters), the federations you confirmed, your seed phrase and the community link.

Your wallet

Your sats are e-cash of a Fedimint federation, held in your browser under your 24 words. No server keeps your balance.

Deposit

Pay a Lightning invoice through a federation gateway, which cannot keep your payment. The smallest deposit is 5 sat, with no fee.

Withdraw

Paste a Lightning invoice and the federation pays it. With several federations, the one holding the most pays.

Balance

Confirmed and pending, shown in the lobby.

Move

Move your e-cash to another federation.

Federations

A federation holds the stakes. Its 7 guardians are Arena nodes, and 5 of 7 must act together to move money. Testnet4 has two, A and B. Every game can be paid through either, and a deposit goes to the less loaded one. A federation the page does not know must be confirmed by you first.

Load

Load is what a federation holds divided by what its guardians' bonds cover. Above 1, mainnet would refuse deposits; testnet4 only warns. The deposit screen shows each one's free room: green below 70 %, yellow from 70 %, red from 90 %. When yours is above 90 % and another is below 50 %, the page offers to move you.

Stake locks and settlement

When you join a paid round, your stake goes into a lock inside the federation, not to anyone. Only three things can open it:

  • You lost: the round's settlement takes it.
  • You won or the round was void: it comes back to you, plus your winnings.
  • Nobody acted: it expires and your wallet takes it back.

The committee signs the settlement together, 5 of 7. There is no pool wallet. Winnings can also go straight to a Lightning address (POST /payout-address).

The games

Three games run on testnet4.

Tick Tock, Next Block

Drive a truck through the next Bitcoin block. Load transactions, drive close to the price war front for points, and deliver before time runs out. Everyone in a round gets the same block. The best run wins.

  • The BTC price comes from a sample the committee signs every 5 seconds, so no single node picks it.
  • Every node replays your inputs and signs the score, so nobody can fake one.
  • The top quarter share the losers' stakes. Other qualified players get their stake back.
  • A round starts every 90 seconds. You can join up to 45 seconds late, but the run still ends with the round.

Rock Paper Scissors

Both players pick a hidden move, then reveal. The winner takes both stakes. A draw returns them.

  • Rounds run every 8 seconds, back to back. A third player gets a second table.
  • If no table is open, the page asks the nodes for one. That takes a while on testnet.
  • If you do not reveal, you lose. If nobody joins your table, you get your stake back.

Bitcoin Up or Down

Will bitcoin be higher at the end of the next 15 minutes? Buy Up or Down. The winning side shares the pot.

  • A new market every 15 minutes. The start and end prices are each a 60-second average. A tie counts as Up.
  • Three independent attestors read the price. 2 of 3 must agree.
  • Orders cost 0.01 to 0.99 a share, at least 5 shares. Unfilled orders can be cancelled for free. A winning share pays 1 sat. The fee is 1 %, only on what fills.
  • If the attestors disagree, or no outcome comes by the deadline, everyone is refunded.

Leaderboard

Each game has a leaderboard of the last 7 days, ranked by net winnings. You can pick 1 to 90 days. The game page shows only players in profit. Refunds and void rounds do not count. Any node serves it: GET /season?game=nextblock&days=7. Add &scope=network for the whole network instead of one node's rounds; a round counts there only when 3 nodes report it the same way, so no single node can add a fake row.

Nostr

Nostr is optional. With it you can:

  • Log in with a browser extension, a phone signer such as Amber, or an nsec. Your wallet follows you to other devices.
  • Pay from your own wallet with Nostr Wallet Connect.
  • Run a node with your Nostr key as the owner key, signing in to the owner area with an extension or remote signer.
  • See results and badges: nodes can publish each round's result and award badges such as First win and Leaderboard top 3. Both are off by default (ARENA_NOSTR_PUBLISH, ARENA_NOSTR_BADGES=1). Our relay is wss://relay.bitcoin-arena.io.
  • Chat with the community on Armada, using the same key.
Bonds, rounds, results and settlement live on Bitcoin and in the federations, not on Nostr. Signing a game manifest in the page needs your 24 words or an nsec.

How Bitcoin Arena works

A developer signs a game or market and publishes it.
Nodes choose which games to host and open rounds.
Players stake sats over Lightning. The federation locks the stake.
The nodes decide the result by replaying each run, or by checking a market's signed outcome.
The federation pays the winners, the nodes and the donations.

Federated

Many independent nodes run the network. There is no server to switch off.

Shared custody

A Fedimint federation holds the sats, not a company or one node. Stakes move only as the result says.

Checked, not trusted

Nodes replay every run. A run that does not replay to the same score is not paid.

No approval

A game exists once its developer signs it. Node operators decide what they host.

Cheating is caught

A false result leaves a proof. The result is overturned and the node loses its bond. When a node cheats

When a node cheats

Nodes are not trusted. Every cheat leaves a proof anyone can check:

The cheatThe proof
Two different results for the same playerThe two signed results
A result the replay does not giveThe inputs, their receipts and the replay
A payout table the rules do not giveThe table and its inputs
Paying out without 5 of 7 signaturesThe settlement record
Signing for a round its own operator played inThe entry and the signature
Claiming fees for work it did not doThe receipt record
Agreeing to a payout, then not signing it in timeIts signed agreement and the expiry

One node cannot do it alone

A result counts only when 5 of the round's 7 seats sign it, so five would have to lie together. See Why 5 of 7.

The objection window

A result waits 10 minutes before it is final. In that time anyone can object with the player's inputs and the nodes' receipts for them (POST /objection). A valid objection stops the result and the replay decides. If 5 seats still cannot agree, the entry is void and the stake goes back. Your app sends your inputs to every node as you play, so the evidence is not only on your phone.

Nodes keep every proof and hand it out (/slash-proofs, /equivocation). After the 10 minutes a proof cannot change the result, but it still stands against every node that signed it.

What happens to the cheater

Every node puts up a bond on Bitcoin. A separate set of arbitration keys can take it, but only with a proof that is named on chain, so anyone can recheck it. Half goes to the harmed players; of the rest, 75% goes to the other active nodes and 25% to the developers. The node then drops out of every future committee. Arbitration can never touch a stake or change a result.

Your money never depends on slashing. The 5-of-7 rule, the objection window and stakes that return to you if nothing is signed protect it. Slashing only punishes.

Today, on testnet4: objections, the re-run, void-and-refund and the kept evidence are running. The slash itself is not: a node only records what it would owe (/slash-owing). Mainnet is not live.

Technical write-ups

Four short pieces on the reasoning, each with its limits.

Why 5 of 7

Every round has its own committee of 7 seats. Nothing counts until 5 sign the same thing.

Seats are drawn from the bonds on Bitcoin, weighted by size, using the hash of a block mined after the draw time, so nobody knows it in advance. Up to 9 nodes are drawn so spares cover no-shows. Your app runs the same draw.

  • Two seats can be gone and the round still finishes.
  • Three honest seats stop a lie, because four signatures are not enough.
  • Two results cannot both pass. Any two groups of 5 share 3 seats, and each of them would have signed both: a proof against them.

Five seats sign the result, then after the 10-minute window that it is final, then the payout table. They settle with one joint signature (FROST), and no seat holds the whole key.

If 5 is not reached, nothing moves. The entry is void, or your stake lock expires and your app takes the stake back. You lose time, not money.

The limit: five nodes lying together can sign a false result. The objection window is there for that, and after it the proof still costs each signer its bond. See When a node cheats. The federation's guardians have their own, separate 5 of 7 (Federations). Check any round with GET /committee and GET /roles.

Your Nostr key is your account

No sign-up, email or password. Log in with a browser extension, a remote signer such as Amber, or a pasted nsec; the page can make a key for you. With an extension or signer the key never reaches the page.

Your wallet's seed comes from the key, or is stored encrypted to it on Nostr relays, so logging in on another device brings the wallet back. The key also gives you your name and picture, Wallet Connect payments, and badges. Nodes publish settled results on Nostr.

The limit: nobody can restore your account. Lose the key and the seed, and the wallet is gone. Money and rounds never run on Nostr. See Nostr for details.

Bonds on Bitcoin

Anyone can run a node, so a seat has a cost: bitcoin locked in a public output. There is no token and no registry. The chain is the list of nodes.

PathWhoWhen
Key pathThe node and the arbitration set together (MuSig2)Any time, for a cooperative release
Path AThe node aloneAfter the bond's maturity height: confirmation + 4,320 blocks on mainnet, + 144 on testnet4
Path B5 of 7 arbitration keysWith a proof of cheating: the slash

A mature bond still counts until it is spent. Every node reads the same bonds and gets the same census, and your app can derive it too.

A slash names the proof on chain (OP_RETURN = H(fraud_proof) ‖ slash_height ‖ hint), so anyone can recheck it. From the 2.0 release, each harmed player gets back their real loss and the rest goes to the protocol reserves, so no one in the round can profit from a cheat. Testnet4 still uses the older split above.

The limits: 5 arbitration keys could take any bond under today's template; a template without that is planned, not built. On testnet4 anyone can derive the arbitration keys, and no live bond has been slashed. Your stake never depends on any of this.

How to write a game

A game is a small program every node runs. You need no permission and no server: stakes, wallet, lobby and payouts already exist.

The one hard rule: seven machines must reach the same score. Use integer maths over the round's seed and the player's inputs only. No clock, no Math.random, no network, no files, no floats.

The template game is coin-count. On a devnet this copies, signs and plays it:

node packages/arena-node/tools/devnet.mjs new-game ./my-game my-game
DEVNET_DIR=./my-devnet DEVNET_APPS=./my-game/game.js node packages/arena-node/tools/devnet.mjs up
node packages/arena-node/tools/devnet.mjs play --game my-game
node packages/arena-node/tools/devnet.mjs replay

Then edit core.js (the rules), body.mjs (the manifest) and game.js (what a node loads), and sign again. Rounds of your game pay the donation set in your manifest (Build and earn).

The limits: operators choose what they host. Paid rounds need the game on the page's list of known games. Devnet deposits into paid rounds do not work yet. Hidden-move games: Implement a head-to-head game. Full walk-through: Implement a game.

4 % of every paid round goes to the nodes that did the work. Every node checks the others: see When a node cheats.

Details

A node is a small Node.js server. The nodes together are Bitcoin Arena; there is no other server. A node lists games and rounds, takes entries, replays runs or checks a market's result, and signs payouts with its committee. It can also hold a key of the money (custody) or give new nodes Lightning liquidity.

The 4 % node fee is four pools of 1 %: verify, settle, custody (guardians ⅔, the two gateways ⅓) and liquidity (by weight). One node can hold several roles.

Details

A new node needs incoming Lightning liquidity to take deposits. A liquidity provider opens a channel of 100,000 sat (testnet4) to it; one or two providers are drawn for each new node.

  • The owner page's Liquidity card shows your coins, channels and the minimum. New address gives you an address to top up.

Bond and advertisement

🔒Bond50,000 sat on Bitcoin
→
⏳ back to you after its lock time🤝 back early together with the arbitration set⚖️ taken only with a proof of cheating
Details

A node gets a seat by posting a bond on Bitcoin. It can be spent three ways:

  • by you alone after its lock time (4,320 blocks on mainnet, 144 on testnet4);
  • by you and the arbitration set together, any time;
  • by the arbitration set alone, only with a proof of cheating (see When a node cheats).

A bond past its lock time still counts until you spend it. The bond's transaction also carries your node's advertisement (roles, address, games), so anyone can find every node on Bitcoin. Changing roles or games is an update, not a new bond.

Details

Nodes post each day's rounds the day before, in a daily order (GET /duty). If a posting fails, the node on duty posts an emergency grid.

  • Your node pays from its Bitcoin Core wallet at 0.1 sat/vB (at most 1 sat/vB); the node fee pays it back. Without a wallet or confirmed coins it skips its turn and says why on the owner page.
  • To turn it off: duty_post=off in arena.conf. duty_wallet, duty_fee_rate and duty_max_fee_rate set the wallet and fees.

Every step in detail
  1. Bitcoin

    Your own Bitcoin Core, an Electrum server from the list, or Install Bitcoin Core for me (Bitcoin Core 31.1 as a service). Disk: mainnet about 950 GB (20 GB pruned), testnet4 about 25 GB (6 GB pruned). Setup goes on once testnet4 is synced.

  2. Roles

    Custody and liquidity need your own Bitcoin Core (not Electrum), LND and coins.

  3. Seed

    24 words, shown once. Write them down: they are your wallet and your owner login.

  4. Owner

    Your 24 words or your Nostr key.

  5. Reach

    Direct on port 8470, a Cloudflare tunnel on your own free account, Tailscale Funnel, a workers.dev name, or a temporary tunnel whose name changes on restart. Setup tests direct first and picks for you. It never opens a port on your router.

  6. Install

    The node runs as a service and you are logged in as its owner.

  7. Lightning (custody only)

    Set up LND here (LND v0.21.5-beta.rc1, wallet from your 24 words) or connect your LND (REST address, admin macaroon, tls.cert). Port 9735 is optional; Tor is bundled. The Fedimint gateway is set up on it. Channel liquidity is yours to add.

    To hold a gateway seat your node must be a guardian with at least 100,000 sat inbound and 100,000 sat outbound, a peg-in of 50,000 sat (mainnet 250,000 / 250,000 / 2,000,000) and 95 % uptime. Seats are redrawn about daily.

    Your guardian joins a new federation automatically once ten custody nodes are ready.

  8. Federation (custody only)

    Nothing to choose: your guardian seat is drawn, never invited.

  9. Bond rules (custody only)

    Switching off without pressing LEAVE on the owner page slashes your bond. You type I UNDERSTAND.

  10. Bond

    Send coins to the address shown: the bond (50,000 sat on testnet4) plus about 2,000 for fees. A custody node adds 363,000 sat (update 5,000, peg-in 50,000, channels 103,000 and 153,000); a liquidity provider adds 500,000. Free testnet4 coins: coinfaucet.eu. The setup then posts the bond and shows when it unlocks.

Notes for Windows, macOS and Linux
  • Windows: bitcoin-arena-node-setup-<version>-windows-x64.exe. No administrator needed. If SmartScreen warns, choose “More info”, then “Run anyway”. If Windows Firewall asks about cloudflared, choose Cancel.
  • macOS: bitcoin-arena-node-<version>-darwin-arm64.dmg (Apple silicon) or -darwin-x64.dmg (Intel). Drag Bitcoin Arena to Applications. The first time, right-click it and choose Open.
  • Linux: bitcoin-arena-node_<version>_amd64.deb or _arm64.deb. Install it with sudo apt install ./bitcoin-arena-node_*.deb, then open Bitcoin Arena or run bitcoin-arena-node-setup.

Updates are automatic. Your node fetches each release, checks its signature, backs itself up, and rolls back if the new release does not start. To update now: bitcoinarena update.

More about updates

auto_update=0 in arena.conf turns auto update off. Then the owner page's Release card tells you when a new release is out, and ./bitcoinarena-install --update <new release> installs it with the same settings.

Several nodes on one machine: run the installer again with --home <folder> --instance <name> --port-offset <n>. Its command is bitcoinarena-<name>. Each node needs its own LND.

Check a release yourself: SHA256SUMS.sig is signed by the key at testnet.bitcoinarena.io/release-key.txt. To build from source: tools/release/build-release.sh <version> <linux|darwin|windows> <x64|arm64> out.

The public dashboard (technical)

Every node has a public /dashboard (and /dashboard.json): earnings, fees by role, the rounds it checked, its settlements, federations and gateways.

The owner area in detail

Open /dashboard/owner on your node. There are no passwords: each request is signed with your owner key, a Nostr key.

  1. Sign in

    Create 24 words, restore them, or use Nostr.

  2. Claim your node

    A new node prints a claim code (XXXX-XXXX-XXXX) in its log. Enter it once; after that only your key can act for the node.

  3. Run it
    • Services: what runs, what is stopped.
    • Standing: your seats and your bond's margin.
    • Bond: when it unlocks. After that, Withdraw sends it to your address. To raise it, run ./bitcoinarena-install --rebond <sat> on the node's machine.
    • Leave or stay: leaving stops new rounds; the node finishes the ones it holds. Leaving is never a slash.
    • Games from other seats: install, update or remove a game.
    • Sign a game or market with your developer key (same 24 words).

More owner requests (bond spends, operator tokens) are in the API reference.

Dev clients and donations

Donations go to the developers' keys in each round's settlement. A developer collects them with the dev client, packages/arena-donor:

arena-donor init       # make the developer's keys
arena-donor once       # collect once
arena-donor loop       # collect every few minutes
arena-donor status     # what is waiting
arena-donor game-key   # the Ed25519 game developer key
arena-donor sign <dir> # sign a game's manifest

It collects from every seat and pays out to your own Lightning node. You can also run a dev client from the owner area: set it up, see what came in, withdraw or remove it.

Run a devnet

A devnet is the whole network on your machine: a private regtest chain, a federation, seven nodes, two Lightning gateways and a free and a paid round. It never touches testnet4 or mainnet.

You need: Node.js 24, Bitcoin Core, the Fedimint build the network runs (with arena-stake-lock from packages/arena-node/tools/stake-lock), and lnd. Without lnd you get free rounds only.

export BITCOIN_BIN=<dir with bitcoind> FEDIMINT_BIN=<dir with fedimintd and arena-stake-lock> LND_BIN=<dir with lnd>
node packages/arena-node/tools/devnet.mjs up        # start everything, print the links
node packages/arena-node/tools/devnet.mjs status    # what runs, its ports, the chain tip, the rounds
node packages/arena-node/tools/devnet.mjs play      # one player through a free round, to its signed result
node packages/arena-node/tools/devnet.mjs replay    # that run replayed here, checked against every node's result
node packages/arena-node/tools/devnet.mjs down      # stop everything it started

down keeps the directory, so the next up is faster. replay [round entry] replays any run; with no arguments, the last play.

DEVNET_BASE_PORTAll ports derive from it (default 24000); they must stay below 32768.
DEVNET_APPSMore games to host (modules that export GAME).

Implement a game

A game is four things: rules (a core every node runs), a client (the page the player sees), a donation key (where your share goes) and a signed manifest. The example is coin-count: each tick the player presses a number, and the score is the sum.

  1. Write the rules as pure arithmetic

    Every node must reach the same score. Use only integers, the seed and the player's inputs: no clock, no Math.random, no network, no files.

    // core.js - coin-count
export const CORE = {
  name: 'coin-count',
  RUN_TICKS: 600,                                   // the longest run a round may ask
  runTicksFits: (t) => Number.isInteger(t) && t >= 1 && t <= 600,
  INPUT_WORDS: 5,                                   // a tick carries five input numbers
  start: (seedWord, config, { runTicks }) => ({ seed: seedWord | 0, sum: 0, ticks: 0, length: runTicks }),
  eventsOf: () => ({ count: { liquidations: 0, loads: 0, samples: 0, tapes: 0 }, before() {} }),
  step(st, words) { if (st.ticks < st.length) { st.sum += words[0] | 0; st.ticks++; } },
  stateHash: (st) => sha256(`coin-count:${st.seed}:${st.sum}:${st.ticks}:${st.length}`),
  result: (st) => ({ outcome: st.ticks < st.length ? null : 'DELIVERED', score: st.sum }),
};
  2. Write the client

    Put it at games/<game name>/index.html. It runs only if its hash is in your manifest. The engine handles stakes, wallet and keys:

    const A = window.parent.ARENA_GAME;
A.rows();          // this game's open rounds: id, line, joinable, entry close
A.identity();      // the player's name and key fingerprint (the same in every game)
A.join(roundId);   // join, through the engine's own join and stake
A.back();          // back to the list of games
  3. Make your donation key 
    node packages/arena-donor/bin/arena-donor.mjs init ./my-game-dev --label "coin-count dev"
# prints recipientPk: the key your game's donation set names
  4. Make your developer key 
    node packages/arena-donor/bin/arena-donor.mjs game-key ./my-game-dev
# writes ./my-game-dev/game-dev.pkcs8 (readable only by you) and prints your public key

    It signs every version of your game. Keep it safe.

  5. Write the manifest 
    // body.mjs - coin-count, version 1
import { RESULT_TYPE, PAYOUT_RULE, NO_PARENT } from './packages/arena-protocol/src/manifest.js';
import { donationSetId } from './packages/arena-protocol/src/donation.js';
const hex = (h) => Uint8Array.from(Buffer.from(h, 'hex'));
export const DONATION_SET = [{ recipientPk: hex('02…your recipientPk…'), label: 'coin-count dev', shareBps: 10000 }];
export const BODY = {
  gameName: 'coin-count', version: 1, parentManifestId: NO_PARENT,
  resultType: RESULT_TYPE.REPLAY, payoutRule: PAYOUT_RULE.RANK_CURVE, rankNumerator: 3, rankDenominator: 4,
  minScore: 1, runTicksMax: 600, roundPeriodMs: 0n,
  cores: [{ hash: hex('…sha256 of core.js…'), configs: [hex('…sha256 of your config…')] }],
  clients: [hex('…sha256 of games/coin-count/index.html…')],
  gameDonationSetId: donationSetId(DONATION_SET),
};
  6. Sign it 
    node packages/arena-donor/bin/arena-donor.mjs sign ./my-game-dev body.mjs
# {"step":"signed","game":"coin-count","version":1,"gameId":"…","file":"./my-game-dev/coin-count-manifest.hex"}

    That id is your game.

  7. Hand it to the nodes

    A node loads a module that exports GAME, listed in its ARENA_APPS:

    // coin-count/game.js
export const GAME = { name: 'coin-count', title: 'Coin Count', manifest, gameId,
  cores: new Map([[CORE_HASH, { name: 'coin-count', hash: CORE_HASH, url: CORE_URL, adapter: CORE,
    configs: new Map([[CONFIG_HASH, {}]]) }]]),
  donationSet: DONATION_SET };

    Then ask operators to host it. Check with GET /games on their nodes.

  8. Test on your own machine

    Use a devnet.

  9. Ship updates

    Sign a new manifest with the same key, version + 1 and the old game id as parentManifestId.

A new game plays free rounds until the page's list of known games includes it.

Payout rules: the rank curve or a top-N split

  • The rank curve (default): the top quarter share the losers' stakes, weighted 0.75rank−1. Other qualifiers get their stake back.
  • A top-N split (PAYOUT_RULE.TOP_SPLIT): fixed shares in basis points summing to 10,000. [10000] is winner takes all, [5000, 3000, 2000] pays three places. Unfilled places' shares go to the filled ones. Requires protocol 4.

Implement a head-to-head game

In a head-to-head game players pick moves in secret, all moves are shown together, and the game's rule decides who won. The engine hides and reveals the moves and moves the money; your game only says which move beats which. Example: Rock Paper Scissors (packages/rock-paper-scissors).

A round, step by step

  1. Hide. Before entry close each player enters with a hash of its move and a fresh salt, so nobody can read or copy it.
  2. Wait. A round seats exactly the game's players; further entries are refused.
  3. Reveal. After entry close each player sends move and salt to every node (POST /reveal). A node accepts it only if it matches the hash.
  4. Decide. Every node gathers all reveals (GET /reveals), applies the rule and signs each player's result.
  5. Pay.
What happenedResultMoney
You wonDELIVERED, score 2your stake plus the losers' stakes
A drawDELIVERED, score 1everyone keeps their stake
You lostFAILEDyour stake goes to the winner
You did not revealFORFEITyour stake goes to the winner
Too few players enteredVOIDevery stake comes back

Build it

  1. Write the rule: one pure function from the revealed moves to WIN, DRAW or LOSS per player. 
    // plugin/decide.js - Rock Paper Scissors: 0 rock, 1 paper, 2 scissors
export const DECIDE = {
  name: 'rock-paper-scissors decide v1',
  decide(moves) {                                   // [{ key, move }]  ->  { [key]: 'WIN' | 'DRAW' | 'LOSS' }
    const shown = [...new Set(moves.map((m) => m.move))];
    const out = {};
    if (shown.length !== 2) { for (const m of moves) out[m.key] = 'DRAW'; return out; }
    const beats = (a, b) => (a - b + 3) % 3 === 1;
    const winning = beats(shown[0], shown[1]) ? shown[0] : shown[1];
    for (const m of moves) out[m.key] = m.move === winning ? 'WIN' : 'LOSS';
    return out;
  },
};

    The manifest lists it in cores[] by its SHA-256.

  2. Write the manifest 
    export const BODY = {
  gameName: 'rock-paper-scissors', version: 1, parentManifestId: NO_PARENT,
  resultType: RESULT_TYPE.HEAD_TO_HEAD,
  cores: [{ hash: sha256(decide.js), configs: [sha256('rock-paper-scissors-config:v1')] }],
  runTicksMax: 0,                                   // no run
  payoutRule: PAYOUT_RULE.RANK_CURVE, rankNumerator: 3, rankDenominator: 4,
  minScore: 2,                                      // a win scores 2, a draw 1
  players: 2,                                       // 2 to 16: a round is played only with all of them
  moveCount: 3,                                     // 2 to 32 moves
  revealWindowMs: 120000n,                          // 10 s to 1 h: the longest a round may give for reveals
  roundPeriodMs: 0n, clients: [sha256(games/rock-paper-scissors/index.html)], gameDonationSetId,
};

    Sign it and hand it to the nodes as in Implement a game.

  3. Write the client: it gets these from window.parent.ARENA_GAME. 
    const A = window.parent.ARENA_GAME;
A.headToHead;          // { players, moveCount } - or null for another kind of game
A.hide(roundId, move); // enter the round with this move hidden
A.hidden(roundId);     // this tab's hidden move in that round (kept in the tab until revealed)
A.hiddenAll();         // every move this tab hid in this game - a round leaves the rows at its entry close
A.reveal(roundId);     // after entry close: send the move and salt to every node
A.reveals(roundId);    // the moves the nodes hold: { [entryId]: move }
A.result(roundId);     // the round as the nodes hold it: entries, each signed outcome and how many nodes signed it

    Show players A.result, the signed result, not your own verdict.

  4. Try it locally 
    ARENA_PAGE=<a page with games/rock-paper-scissors/index.html> node packages/rock-paper-scissors/tools/demo-regtest.mjs ./work
# a private test chain, two nodes hosting both games, one free round always open; prints the lobby link

    Open the link in two browser profiles and add &game=rock-paper-scissors.

Implement a prediction market

Players pick one outcome; the right picks share the wrong picks' stakes. There is no core and no run: the outcome comes from the attestors you name.

  1. Choose the outcomes and who reports: 2 to 16 outcomes, 1 to 16 attestors (Ed25519 keys) and how many must agree, for example 2 of 3.
  2. Write the manifest 
    export const BODY = {
  gameName: 'btc-up-down', version: 1, parentManifestId: NO_PARENT,
  resultType: RESULT_TYPE.ATTESTED,          // the result is an outcome, not a replayed run
  payoutRule: PAYOUT_RULE.PARI_MUTUEL,       // the right picks share the wrong picks' stakes
  outcomes: ['up', 'down'],
  attestors: [KEY_A, KEY_B, KEY_C],          // 32-byte Ed25519 public keys
  attestThreshold: 2,                        // 2 of the 3 must sign the same outcome
  cores: [], runTicksMax: 0,                 // nothing to run
  clients: [hex('…sha256 of games/btc-up-down/index.html…')],
  gameDonationSetId: donationSetId(DONATION_SET),
  // … roundPeriodMs and the rest as for a game
};

    Sign it and hand it to the nodes as for a game.

  3. Players pick and stake: an entry carries the index of one outcome.
  4. Report the outcome: after entry close the attestors sign it and anyone posts it to the nodes. 
    import { createPrivateKey, sign } from 'node:crypto';
import { attestationBodyId, encodeAttestation } from './packages/arena-protocol/src/attested.js';

const body = { roundId, outcome: 0, observedAtMs: BigInt(Date.now()) };   // 0 = 'up'
const digest = Buffer.from(attestationBodyId(body));
const signatures = [attestorA, attestorB].map((a) => ({
  key: a.publicKey,                                                     // 32 bytes
  signature: new Uint8Array(sign(null, digest, a.privateKey)),          // Ed25519, 64 bytes
}));
const attestation = encodeAttestation({ ...body, signatures });

for (const node of committeeNodes)
  await fetch(`${node}/outcome?round=${roundIdHex}`, { method: 'POST', body: attestation });
  5. Follow it: GET /outcome?round=… answers OPEN, ATTESTED or DISPUTED. A result settles after standing 600 s uncontested. Two signed, different outcomes make the round DISPUTED and every stake is refunded.

How a market pays out

  • Fees are set in the manifest: 0.5% to the nodes and 0.5% donation by default, at most 5% each and 10% together. A 1 sat pick at 1% stakes 0.99 sat.
  • Fees are due when an order fills, at entry close. Before that a player can cancel for free (POST /cancel). After resolution the nodes take their part and the donation goes to the protocol and market developers.
  • There is no house: the wrong picks pay the right ones.
  • Each winning entry gets its net stake back plus losing pool × its net stake ÷ all winning net stakes, rounded down. Leftover millisats go one each to the winners.
  • If nobody picked the winner, or the market is void, everything goes back, fees included.

Bitcoin Up or Down, every 15 minutes

One market per quarter hour. Up wins if the time-weighted average BTC price over the window is at or above the start price; a tie is Up.

Trading uses signed limit orders at 0.01 to 0.99, at least 5 shares; a share pays 1 sat if its side wins. An Up buyer at 0.73 and a Down buyer at 0.27 fund a share together. Fees are charged on the filled part; the rest can be cancelled for free.

Event-market templates

A template is one signed set of market rules whose rounds each ask their own question: 1 to 280 bytes, 2 to 16 outcome labels of 1 to 64 bytes, and a resolution time inside the template's window. Attestors sign an index into that round's labels. GET /rounds and GET /outcome carry the question and labels.

Markets on games

Spectators back BULLS or BEARS of one Tick Tock round, and that round's signed result decides the market. No attestors. Entries close before the round starts, and the round must end by the market's deadline, or the market is void. FLAT, or no result by the deadline, refunds every stake.

Scalar markets

A market on a number between two bounds. Players pick LONG or SHORT. Attestors sign the value, clamped to the bounds. LONG gets (value - low) ÷ (high - low) of the pool, SHORT the rest. One-sided, missing or contradicted values refund every stake.

Protocol reserves

The network's own fund, spent only on what bonded nodes vote for.

  • Each federation holds its share under a key that 5 of its 7 guardians must sign.
  • Every node shows them at GET /reserves; bitcoinarena.io shows the total.
  • If a federation closes, its reserves move to a healthy one automatically.

The referral program

A website that brings players earns 10% of the nodes' fee on their stakes. The player pays nothing extra: on a 10,000 sat stake the nodes' 400 sat fee splits 40 to the website, 360 to the nodes.

  • Create a program in your node's owner area under Referrer; it gets its own key. Each entry signs that key in, so no node can swap it.
  • Link to https://testnet.bitcoinarena.io/?ref=<key> (the page keeps it 30 days) or embed a game with /?game=rock-paper-scissors&ref=<key>. See the demo page.
  • Withdraw over Lightning from Referrer. Earnings show on the Metrics page and at GET /referral-fees.

A fee proposal can set the share between 0 and 40%. Guide: Referral Program.

Funding proposals: how the reserves pay out

Anyone can propose a payout: purpose, amount and Lightning address, signed with your key.

  1. Signal (1 week)

    It goes to a vote if nodes making up at least 15% of the recently active nodes support it. Only nodes with a bond at least 3 months old that served rounds in 3 of the last 4 weeks count. Nothing is paid on a signal.

  2. Vote (2 weeks)

    Bonded nodes vote YES, NO or ABSTAIN on Bitcoin. It passes when 60% of the votes say YES and YES is at least 20% of everyone who could vote.

  3. Payout

    After 2 more weeks, the federation pays the Lightning address; a failed payment returns to the reserves.

Five rules, so nobody can rent a majority.

  1. Only bonds at least 3 months old, locked at least 3 months after the vote, can vote.
  2. Votes weigh bond × age: 1× under a year, 2× from one year, 3× from two.
  3. 60% YES, and YES at least 20% of all who could vote.
  4. At most 10% of the reserves per proposal, 25% per 3 months.
  5. 2 weeks wait after the vote, when NO votes can stop it. Above 1% of the reserves it pays in 4 monthly parts, each stoppable the same way.

No vote can pass in a network's first 3 months. Every payout is recorded with its proof.

Discuss and propose on the Project proposals forum, or send it signed to any node with POST /funding. GET /funding lists every proposal with its comments, signals and vote.

FAQ

Where are my sats?

As e-cash in your browser wallet. The federation's guardians hold the bitcoin behind it, and most of them must act together to move it.

I lost my 24 words. Can anyone recover my wallet?

No. Back them up in Settings.

How do I get testnet sats?

From the test Lightning wallet the page links. For a node's bond, coinfaucet.eu sends free testnet4 coins.

What is the smallest deposit?

5 sat.

Why does my federation show a load above 1?

It holds more than its guardians' bonds cover. On testnet4 that is only a warning.

Federation A or B?

You don't have to choose. The page picks for you.

Are there free rounds?

No, only paid ones. Free rounds are for developers on a devnet.

What is taken from a paid stake?

10 %: 4 % to the nodes, 2 % to the game's developer, 2 % to the protocol developers and 2 % to the reserves. Up or Down takes 1 % instead. See Where the money goes.

Who can ask for money from the reserves?

Anyone, with a proposal the bonded nodes vote on. See Funding proposals and testnet.bitcoinarena.io/proposals.

What if a node cheats?

One node decides nothing. 5 of 7 must sign the same result, and anyone can object for 10 minutes. If they can't agree, you get your stake back. See When a node cheats.

What if nobody settles my round?

Your lock expires and your wallet takes it back.

Can I join a round that has started?

Yes, while entries are open. You just get less time.

Rock Paper Scissors: what if my opponent does not reveal?

You win.

Do I need Nostr?

No. A 24-word wallet works without it.

How do I claim my node?

Open /dashboard/owner on it and enter the claim code from the node's log.

Is this on mainnet?

No, testnet4 only. Test sats have no value.
